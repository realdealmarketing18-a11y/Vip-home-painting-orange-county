## THE SWATCH-SCALE ARGUMENT — the most valuable single finding

This deserves its own heading because it changes what the page should say.

The current copy says: *"A paint chip in a store tells you almost nothing about how that color
will look on your own house, in your own light."* True, but it aims at the **lazy** buyer — the
one who grabbed a chip at the store.

The real customer is the **opposite** of lazy. The Houzz poster did everything the industry
tells you to do — and the industry's own best practice failed her anyway. The stronger claim,
and the one the evidence supports, is:

> **Doing it the careful way does not work either.** Weeks of samples on three exposures still
> produced a house she felt sick about. Small samples cannot predict large surfaces. The only
> thing that predicts your whole house is *your whole house.*

That reframes the visualizer from a convenience ("saves you time") to a **category correction**
("the standard method is broken, and here is the only one that isn't"). Convenience is a
feature. A category correction is a position.

### The gap statement
*Every painter in Orange County sells the quality of the brushstroke. Not one of them sells
certainty about the color before the brush is loaded — and the industry's own recommended
method, testing swatches, is the thing that failed the homeowner in the first place.*

### Three hook candidates
*Each drawn from a real line above, cited.*

1. **"She did everything right. The house still came out wrong."**
   → from *"I really determined not to make a rookie mistake on this, and yet here I am"* + the
   2×2 swatch line. Leads with the careful buyer, not the careless one.

2. **"A 2-by-2 swatch cannot tell you what 3,000 square feet will look like."**
   → from *"now I know I should have painted even bigger swatches?"* The most literal, most
   provable, most category-correcting line available. Strongest candidate for the page.

3. **"The second paint job costs exactly what the first one did."**
   → from *"it is so permanent"* + VIP's own published $4.75/sq ft. Already near-present in the
   page copy ("A color you regret costs the whole job twice") — the research confirms it and
   supplies the dollar figure to make it concrete.
