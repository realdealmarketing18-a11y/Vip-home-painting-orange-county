## FINDING 6 — COLOR IS AN ENERGY DECISION, AND NOBODY HERE SAYS SO ⭐

**Light Reflectance Value (LRV)** runs 0 (absolute black) to 100 (pure white) and states the
percentage of light a color reflects. Higher LRV reflects more light and therefore absorbs
less heat; lower LRV absorbs more and can raise indoor temperature.
— [Wikipedia: Light reflectance value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_reflectance_value) ·
[Shoreline Painting](https://shorelinepaintingct.com/blog/light-reflectance-value-and-paint/) ·
[A Step Above Painting](https://www.asapsrq.com/the-science-of-light-reflectance-value-choosing-colors-for-energy-efficiency/)

**Why this is the single best fit for this page.** The whole page is about choosing a color.
LRV makes that choice an *energy* decision as well as an aesthetic one — and VIP already
publishes every scheme's Sherwin-Williams colors, so the LRV of each is a lookup, not a
guess. No competitor in the local pack connects color to cooling cost.

**The benefit, in her terms:** *the color you pick changes what the house costs to cool.*

**`[VERIFY]` — do not publish these two until sourced better:**
- "lower indoor temperature by as much as 3 °C" — traced to a patent filing, not a
  peer-reviewed or utility source.
- "24% annual energy saving on a Los Angeles test building" — same patent document
  ([USPTO 7157112](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7157112)).
  A number this good needs a better citation than a patent before it goes near a sales page.

**Owed:** the actual LRV of all 11 schemes' body colors, pulled from Sherwin-Williams. That
turns a general principle into VIP's own data, which is what makes it citable.

## FINDING 7 — IR-REFLECTIVE PIGMENT KILLS THE OBJECTION TO EVERY DARK SCHEME ⭐⭐

**This is the most valuable finding in this run.**

VIP sells dark directions — Obsidian Monolith, Riviera Tuxedo, Euro-Industrial Estate. The
standing objection to all three is *dark colors make the house hot*. That objection now has
a manufacturer answer:

> Solar reflective pigments are "altered physically and chemically to reflect infrared
> radiation while absorbing the same amount of visible light… **solar reflective pigments can
> help darker colors keep a surface cooler.**"
> — [Sherwin-Williams, Cool Coatings](https://industrial.sherwin-williams.com/na/us/en/coil-extrusion/sustainability/cool-roof-wall-coatings.html)

**And there is a product for exactly this substrate.** `Loxon XP IR Reflective Coating`
integrates masonry and concrete protection with infrared technology that reflects solar rays
back into the atmosphere — masonry and concrete meaning **stucco**, which is what Orange
County estates are.
— [Sherwin-Williams: Loxon XP IR Reflective](https://www.sherwin-williams.com/homeowners/products/loxon-xp-ir-reflective-coating-pcp-28111) ·
[AECinfo announcement](https://www.aecinfo.com/sherwin-williams-new-loxon-xp-ir-reflective-coating-offers-advanced-infrared-73800/news.html)

Stated results: lower exterior surface temperature, less heat gain, reduced energy
consumption, cooler interiors.

**The benefit:** *you can have the dark, dramatic elevation without paying for it in August.*
That is a benefit no competitor in this market is offering, it is defensible because the
manufacturer says it, and it converts VIP's boldest schemes from a risk into a feature of
the spec.

**Strategic note:** this pairs with the visualizer perfectly. She can *see* the dark scheme
on her house (removing the aesthetic risk) and be told the coating answers the heat
(removing the practical one). Both objections, same screen.

## FINDING 8 — TITLE 24 IS ABOUT ROOFS. DO NOT CLAIM IT COVERS WALLS. ⚠️

**This corrects an assumption made before the research ran.**

Title 24 Part 6 sets cool-surface minimums by climate zone — aged solar reflectance, thermal
emittance, Solar Reflectance Index — and the residential prescriptive requirements are for
**roofs**. For Climate Zones 1–9 residential re-roofs: low-slope needs 3-year aged solar
reflectance ≥ 0.63 and thermal emittance ≥ 0.75; steep-slope ≥ 0.20 and ≥ 0.75.
— [Cool Roof Rating Council: California Title 24](https://coolroofs.org/resources/california-title-24) ·
[CRRC 2022 summary (PDF)](https://coolroofs.org/documents/2022-Title-24-Part-6-Cool-Roof-Summary.pdf)

**No residential cool-WALL prescriptive requirement was found.** Searches returned roof
requirements only.

**So the rule is:** never imply Title 24 requires anything of exterior walls, and never imply
a repaint brings a house into compliance. It does not. Saying so would be a false regulatory
claim on a page that sells honesty about what you can and cannot see.

**`[VERIFY]` if we want this angle at all:** whether cool walls exist as an optional
compliance *credit* in the performance path. That needs the California Energy Commission's
own Title 24 Part 6 text, not a roofing vendor's blog. Until then the angle is **dropped**.
