### 3 · Back up your big promise
After the big-promise headline, back it up with a straightforward explanation in the
**subheadline**.

### 4 · Create irresistible intrigue
Headline demands attention, subheadline positions the promise — **now dial up intrigue with
bullets**. Write **10 to 20** additional headlines and trim to your **best six**. Each bullet
calls out the pains, fears, hopes and dreams of the target market and adds intrigue —
*"The Little-Known Secret to ____ that ____ Don't Want You to Know."*

### 5 · Shine a floodlight on the problem
*Fabian's margin note: **bullet points = money sauce**.*

Identify the audience — who they are, how they feel — or tell a story about a problem, a
struggle, a challenge. Explain **vividly** how it feels to have the specific problem. They
should feel you are reading their mind and be left saying **"Yeah, that's exactly how I
feel."** Agitate it so they feel the pain and become motivated to act. **People are much more
motivated by moving away from pain than towards pleasure.**

Then describe in vivid detail **what they've tried before and why it didn't work.**

> `07-VOICE.md`'s "what she's already tried and why it failed" table was built for exactly
> this section — five attempts, each with her own words about it.

### 6 · Provide the solution
*Fabian's margin notes: **headline + subheadline**, **SEO**, **services**.*

Reveal a solution to their problem with your products or services, then **prove this solution
is the best viable option that exists.** Demonstrate clearly how it is **different from all
the other solutions they have tried which failed.**

### 7 · Show your credentials
Reader scepticism is high here — *"Sure he says he can fix my problem. That's what they all
say."* Prove you can be trusted:

- Results achieved · successful case studies · prestigious companies or people you've worked
  with · number of customers served · press mentions · awards or recognitions

They must feel **"you've been there and done that"** and that they can expect the same.

### 8 · Detail the benefits
**"People don't care about you or your product or service, they only care about what it will
do for them. Features tell and benefits sell, so talk only in benefits."** Use bullet points.

Make a **two-column list**: features in one, the corresponding benefit in the other.

### 9 · Social proof
Build credibility with **third-party validation** — research statistics, or quotes from
credible or authoritative sources.

### 10 · Make your Godfather Offer
To convert, the offer must be:

- **Clear and easy to understand** — no question what they get in return
- **Value-based** — how it fills a need or solves a problem
- **Concise** — short drives more conversions
- **Persuasive** — the one place to use everything you have
- **Irresistible** — a no-brainer, leaving them asking *"How can they possibly offer so much
  value?"* **So compelling only a lunatic would refuse.**

### 11 · Add bonuses
Relevant bonuses or sweeteners. Should be **highly desirable but not essential** to reaching
the desired outcome — they simply need to want them.

### 12 · Stack the value
Stack the total value and benefits of everything in the offer. Tell them what everything is
worth, then paint a vivid picture with benefit explanations to raise perceived value.

### 13 · Reveal your price
Add the prices together to calculate value, then reveal a price much cheaper. Explain why the
price is what it is and why it is such great value. **If the goal is lead generation and you
are pitching a free consult, put a dollar value on what the consult is worth** — not to be
confused with the price of your services, which is covered on the call.

### 14 · Inject scarcity
Offers without scarcity don't sell as well, **but it must be genuine or you'll destroy your
reputation.** Examples: expiration date · countdown clock · "doors are closing" · "only X left
at this price" · "buy before X to avoid a price hike."

### 15 · Give a powerful guarantee
Remove, eliminate, reverse and take out perceived risk. **Longer guarantee = fewer returns.**
A guarantee transfers risk from buyer to seller and shows they won't be at a loss of time or
money — eliminating the pain of buying. A money-back guarantee **or a promise not to share
their information** both qualify.

### 16 · Call to action
**The CTA is a command.** Be specific, tell them exactly what to do, keep it clear and direct.
**Ask them to do just one thing** — the more hoops, the more likely they say "screw this" and
leave.

### 17 · Close with a P.S. — a warning and a reminder
Always include a closing P.S. **It is the third most read element of the letter.** Remind them
of the irresistible offer, **warn them of the consequence of not acting**, include the call to
action, and remind them of the limited time or quantity.
