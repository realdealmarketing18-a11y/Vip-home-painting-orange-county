Notes on this screen

Why the photo comes before the contact fields

The name and contact block is hidden until a photo is attached. Adding a photo is the interesting part and costs nothing emotionally; typing your number into a painter’s website does. Doing it in that order means the ask lands on someone who has already started, not on someone deciding whether to.

Why their photo lands in the gold plate

It is the same frame the estate sat in through Steps One to Four. The moment their own house appears there, the service stops being a claim about what we do and becomes a picture of it. That swap is the entire point of the screen.

The shot rules are UX, not fine print

Daylight, square to the facade, whole front in frame, nothing parked in the way — those are the intake rejection rules out of VIZ‑RENDER.md, said where they prevent the problem instead of in a document nobody on the site will read. A bad source photo cannot be rescued by a good prompt.

What is deliberately missing

No phone field forced alongside email, no “project budget” dropdown, no “how did you hear about us”. Every extra field costs completions, and none of those answers changes what gets rendered. The phone number stays visible for the few who are ready to talk now — they should never have to use a form to reach you.

Two things still unset

The turnaround. The button reads [turnaround] on purpose. That number comes out of test T4 — running one house end to end and timing it — not out of a guess. It is a promise printed on a button, and the moment it slips it damages the one position no competitor can copy.

The endpoint. Submitting is mocked here so the screen can be judged. Wiring it to WordPress or a hosted endpoint is a one-line change to the form’s action; nothing about this layout depends on which.
