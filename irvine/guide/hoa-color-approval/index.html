How to Get Your Exterior Color Approved by an Irvine HOA Without Three Rounds of Revisions

Who actually decides, what to have ready, and the mistake that sends most applications back.

Most exterior color applications in Irvine are not rejected. They are returned — for a missing document, an unclear sample, or a submittal sent to the wrong body entirely. Each return costs weeks, and the weeks are the part homeowners actually mind.

The process is not difficult. It is just specific, and it differs by village.

The short answer

Exterior color changes in Irvine’s master-planned villages generally require written approval from the community association’s architectural or design review body before work begins. Getting through in one round comes down to four steps:

  • Identify the exact association that governs your property — several Irvine villages contain more than one
  • Obtain that association’s current architectural guidelines and treat them, not general advice, as the authority
  • Submit a complete package the first time, with the colors clearly identified by manufacturer name and code
  • Do not start work until written approval is in hand, regardless of how routine the change seems

Step one: find out which association actually governs your home

This sounds trivial and it is where a surprising number of submittals go wrong.

The City of Irvine publishes an official lookup tool that maps a property to its homeowners association and management company. It is a government primary source, and it is more reliable than a listing site, a neighborhood forum, or the assumption that your village has one association.

Start at cityofirvine.gov/community-development/irvine-homeowners-associations.

  • Orchard Hills — multiple separate associations, each with its own board
  • Stonegate — Stonegate Village Owners Association (SVOA). Note that a separate, unrelated 126-unit "Stonegate Homeowners Association, Inc." exists elsewhere; they are not the same entity
  • Woodbury — Woodbury Community Association, one of the largest in Irvine at 4,067 units
  • Portola Springs — Portola Springs Association
  • Altair — Altair Irvine Master Association
  • Hidden Canyon — self-presented at livinghiddencanyon.com

Orchard Hills is not one association. The Groves, The Reserve and The Summit each have their own board and their own review. Submitting to the wrong one costs you the full cycle.

Step two: the guidelines are the authority, not anyone’s advice

Every association sets its own architectural standards, and they are not interchangeable between villages — or, in Orchard Hills, between associations inside the same village.

Request the current architectural guidelines and the CC&Rs from your management company in writing, and read the section on exterior modifications before you fall in love with a palette. What is approvable in one Irvine village may not be in the next, and guidelines are revised periodically, so a neighbor’s successful submittal from four years ago is not a reliable guide.

Anyone who tells you what your HOA will accept without having read your association’s current guidelines is guessing. That includes painters.

Step three: what to have ready before you submit

Requirements vary by association, so the guidelines you obtained in step two govern. That said, applications generally turn on being able to show the reviewers precisely what they are approving — which means unambiguous color identification and a clear picture of the result.

The items below are what VIP assembles as part of the project, at no additional cost. If you are managing the submittal yourself, they are a reasonable starting checklist to compare against your association’s actual form.

  • The association’s current architectural application form, completed in full
  • Every color identified by manufacturer, name and code — for example Sherwin-Williams Alabaster SW 7008 — never by description alone
  • Physical color samples or manufacturer chips, as the guidelines specify
  • A clear indication of which color goes on which element: body, trim, fascia, garage door, entry door, accents
  • Photographs of the existing elevations
  • A rendering of the proposed colors on your actual home, where the association accepts one
  • Contractor license and insurance information, if the form requests it

The mistake that costs most people a round

It is not choosing a bold color. It is submitting something the committee cannot evaluate with confidence.

A reviewer looking at three paper chips stapled to a form has to imagine the result on your elevation, in your village’s light, next to your roof. Faced with genuine uncertainty, a committee’s safest decision is to ask for more information — which is a return, and another cycle.

Giving them the finished picture removes that uncertainty. It is the same reason the visualization matters to you: the difference between a decision made on imagination and a decision made on sight.

The second most common mistake is starting work before written approval arrives. Verbal encouragement from a board member is not approval, and unwinding unapproved work is far more expensive than waiting.

Questions people ask

Do I need HOA approval to repaint my house in Irvine?+
In Irvine’s master-planned villages, exterior color changes generally require written approval from the community association’s architectural or design review body before work begins. Requirements are set by each association individually, so the governing document is your own association’s current architectural guidelines. Confirm which association governs your property using the City of Irvine homeowners association lookup.
How do I find out which HOA my Irvine home belongs to?+
The City of Irvine publishes an official lookup tool that maps a property to its homeowners association and management company, at cityofirvine.gov/community-development/irvine-homeowners-associations. This matters more than it sounds: several Irvine villages contain more than one association. Orchard Hills alone includes The Groves, The Reserve and The Summit, each with its own board and review process.
Does VIP Home Painting handle the HOA submittal?+
Yes. Preparing your community association’s design review package is included as part of the project rather than charged separately. That covers identifying colors by manufacturer and code, documenting which color goes on which element, and providing a rendering of the proposed palette on your actual home where the association accepts one.
What happens if I paint without approval?+
Associations can require unapproved work to be brought back into compliance, which in the case of exterior color means repainting the house at your own cost. Verbal encouragement from a board member or neighbor is not approval. Wait for the written decision — the delay is far cheaper than the alternative.
How long does architectural approval take in Irvine?+
Timelines are set by each association and published in its own governing documents, so we will not quote a number that may not apply to your village. Request the review schedule and meeting calendar from your management company at the same time you request the guidelines. What is within your control is submitting a complete package the first time, since returns add a full cycle.
What to remember
  1. Confirm your exact association through the City of Irvine lookup before anything else.
  2. Get the current guidelines in writing. They are the authority; a neighbor’s old approval is not.
  3. Identify every color by manufacturer, name and code, and say which element each one goes on.
  4. Submit complete the first time — returns cost weeks, not days.
  5. Never start work before written approval is in hand.
