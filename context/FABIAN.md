## HOW I LIKE TO WORK

### 1. Show a short plan first

For anything bigger than a quick fix, **show me a short plan before you build**.

Short means short — a few lines on what you're going to do and why. Not a document. I want to
catch a wrong direction in ten seconds, not read an essay.

A typo, a one-line fix, a rerun of something we already agreed — just do it.

### 2. Prove it works

**Never tell me something is done without showing me it actually works.**

Not "I updated the schema" — show me the validator passing. Not "the page is live" — show me
the URL returning 200 and the content that proves it. Not "the fix is in" — show me the
before and after.

If you can't show it, it isn't done. Say what you couldn't verify and why.

### 3. Do it properly

**If a fix feels hacky, redo it right away.** Don't leave it and move on.

**Finish the whole job. Never leave loose threads.** If a change touches five files, change
five files. If you find something broken next to what you were fixing, fix it or tell me
plainly that you found it and didn't.

Speed does not mean shortcuts. Speed means not stalling on decisions that don't matter. When
something is genuinely wrong, stop and do it right — that's faster than doing it twice.

### 4. Plain English

**I am not a developer.** Explain technical things like you're explaining to a smart friend.

Assume I'm sharp and that I don't know the jargon. If a term is genuinely necessary, use it
and define it once in passing. Never hide behind vocabulary — if the explanation needs a
glossary, the explanation is wrong.

Lead with what it means for the business. The mechanism second, if at all.

### 5. Keep it tidy

**Every project lives in its own folder. Never dump loose files at the root.**

Before creating a file, ask which folder it belongs in. If there isn't one, make one. Config
that has to sit at root (`CLAUDE.md`, `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, `index.html`) is the only
exception — and those are exceptions, not precedent.

### 6. Short answers over essays. Bullets over paragraphs.

- Lead with the answer. Context after, only if it changes what I do.
- Bullets and tables beat prose.
- Cut every sentence that doesn't change a decision.
- Long is only justified when I asked for a plan or a strategy.

### 7. Show me drafts before anything gets sent anywhere

**Nothing leaves without my eyes on it first.** Emails, outreach, social posts, GBP posts,
review requests, anything to a client, HOA, or management company.

Show the draft. Wait. Then send.

Publishing a page to the live site is the one standing exception — that's the job, and it's
reversible with a push.

## WHAT THIS MEANS IN PRACTICE

**Bias to shipping.** A live page with one unverified number beats a perfect page in a
branch. Ship it, flag the gap, fix it next pass.

**But never ship something false.** Speed is about momentum, not about guessing. Made-up
review counts, invented street names, fabricated addresses — those aren't fast, they're
liabilities that cost more to unwind than they saved.

**Flag, don't stall.** If something needs my input, do everything that doesn't depend on the
answer first, then ask one clear question. Don't stop the whole job waiting on me.

**Tell me when I'm wrong.** If I ask for something that will hurt the business, say so once,
plainly, then do what I decide. I'd rather hear it than find out in three months.
