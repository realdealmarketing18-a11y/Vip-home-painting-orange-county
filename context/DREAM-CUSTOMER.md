# DREAM CUSTOMER — who we target, and how it changes the research

Loaded by Marcus and the Copywriter. **This document exists to change decisions.** Every
section ends with what to actually do differently.

---

## THE CORE INSIGHT

**Our buyer is not price-sensitive. They are regret-averse.**

They can afford the repaint. What they cannot afford is spending five figures on a color they
end up hating, on a house their neighbors look at every day, in a community where standards
are visible from the sidewalk.

Everything follows from this. We do not sell paint, or even craftsmanship. **We sell the
removal of a decision risk.**

→ **Do differently:** never lead with price or discounts. Lead with certainty.

---

## BUYER 1 — THE HOMEOWNER (B2C)

Owners of $1.5M–$9M+ homes in design-review, often guard-gated communities. Frequently
dual-income professionals, established families, or empty-nesters upgrading in place.

**What they're actually buying:** confidence that the result will look the way they imagined,
and that the process won't disrupt their household or embarrass them with the neighbors.

**What they fear**
- Choosing a color that looks wrong at full scale — the single biggest fear
- Being judged: by neighbors, by the HOA, by themselves every time they pull in the driveway
- Crews who treat a $4M estate like a tract repaint — overspray, mess, strangers, noise
- A vague lump-sum quote that grows mid-project

**What convinces them**
- Seeing their *own* home in the color before committing
- Itemization — it reads as competence, not cheapness
- Evidence we understand their specific community's architecture and light
- Someone else handling the design-review paperwork

**Where they look:** Google organic (not the map pack — they research, they don't
"near me"), Nextdoor and neighborhood groups, referrals, Houzz/Yelp for verification.

→ **Do differently:** capture verified street names, real architecture, and actual light
conditions per community. Generic luxury language is invisible to this buyer — specificity
*is* the proof.

### They are not one audience — the spread is 5×

| Tier | Communities | What they actually worry about |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · gated** | Hidden Canyon $9.15M · Altair $4.59M · Orchard Hills $3.51M | Discretion. Gate access, crew conduct, schedule around the household. They likely already employ a designer. **Price is not the objection.** |
| **2** | Stonegate $1.82M · Portola Springs · Woodbury | Value and certainty. Doing it once, correctly. Itemization matters more here. |

→ **Do differently:** if a community's median is under ~$1.2M, deprioritize it — the
visualization premium is a harder sell and the project sizes don't justify the page.

## BUYER 2 — THE HOA BOARD / PROPERTY MANAGER (B2B)

Association boards and the management companies serving them, buying **common-area** painting
on recurring maintenance cycles.

**Why this may be the bigger prize:** in Irvine, **one** company (Keystone Pacific) manages at
least four of six target villages, including Woodbury Community Association at **4,067
units**. One relationship reaches thousands of units. **No competitor in either market
markets to HOAs.**

**What they're buying:** clubhouse, pool houses, perimeter walls, monuments, guard houses,
park structures, mailbox kiosks, wrought iron — on a reserve-study schedule that already
budgets repainting.

**What they fear**
- Choosing a color hundreds of residents will complain about
- A bid that doesn't survive scrutiny, or a contractor who can't document insurance
- Work that disrupts residents or drags past the window
- Being personally blamed — they spend other people's money and get voted out over bad calls

**What convinces them**
- **Documentation:** itemized bids, insurance certificates, licensing, defined work windows
- **Visualization as governance** — a board voting on a clubhouse color can show residents the
  rendered result first. That removes the political risk from the vote. This is a *stronger*
  pitch to a board than to a homeowner.
- Evidence of working inside guard-gated communities without incident

→ **Do differently:** the CTA is **"Request a bid,"** never "book a consultation." Boards
procure; they don't shop. And research the **management company**, not just the association —
the manager is the gatekeeper and one relationship compounds.

## WHAT WE ARE NOT CHASING

- Price shoppers, three-bids-lowest-wins
- Rentals, flips, quick turnarounds
- Commercial and industrial
- Communities where the median home is under ~$1.2M

→ **Do differently:** if research shows a market competing mainly on price, note it and
deprioritize the cluster.

## HOW THIS SHAPES RESEARCH — the checklist

1. **Prefer guard-gated, design-review communities** — high value, high regret-aversion, thin competition
2. **Always capture the HOA/design-review process** — nobody else writes about it
3. **Verify streets and landmarks** — specificity is the proof of local knowledge
4. **Capture architecture precisely** — palette advice must fit the actual housing stock
5. **Capture median home value** — it sets the tier and therefore the entire register
6. **Research the management company, not just the association**
7. **Note anything price-led** — that's a signal to deprioritize
