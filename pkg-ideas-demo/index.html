Package shot · proposal, not shipped

Three changes to the package section, side by side

Left is what is on the page right now. Right is the proposal. Every image is a real VIP render — the same eleven schemes the live section uses. Nothing here has been committed to the sales page.

01

Put the count on the picture

The page says eleven schemes in the copy, but nowhere on screen does she ever feel eleven. A counter turns a claim into a quantity she is moving through. Click the thumbnails in both panels — the difference is what the top-left corner tells you.

On the page now
Proposed
Why it works: she arrives at scheme four and the corner says 04 / 11 — which means seven more are waiting. The counter creates the same pull a page number does in a catalogue. Costs nothing: no new renders, no credits, about fifteen lines of CSS.
02

Say out loud that this is not her house

Every render in this section is the Gallagher villa in Newport Beach. The headline promises “On Your House” — and then shows someone else’s. She notices. Naming the gap before she can object turns a weak spot into the offer.

On the page now

Every one of these is the same house. Tap any one to see it larger

She is left to work out on her own whose house she is looking at, and whether she will get the same thing.

Proposed

Every one of these is the same house. Tap any one to see it larger This one belongs to the Gallaghers in Newport Beach. Yours gets the same eleven — on your own front elevation.

One sentence. It answers the objection she is already forming, and it restates the offer at the exact moment she is most convinced.

Why it works: the strongest version of a proof shot admits what it is. Saying “this is theirs, yours is next” is more persuasive than hoping she does not ask — and it is the difference between a portfolio and an offer. Copy only.
03

Move the $329 next to the eleven

The $329 anchor is the single most persuasive number on the page, and right now it sits below the renders. She sees eleven schemes, scrolls past them, and only then reads what three cost elsewhere. The comparison should happen while she is still looking at the eleven.

On the page now
↓   she scrolls past the renders   ↓

Every one of these is the same house.

↓   and keeps scrolling   ↓

What three color variations cost elsewhere: $329. An independent color consultant’s exterior package arrives as a written report with three variations and a set of paint cards in the mail.

By the time she reads “three for $329”, the eleven are off her screen. She has to hold them in memory to feel the contrast.

Proposed

Three variations cost $329 elsewhere. A color consultant’s exterior package is a written report with three options and paint cards in the mail. You are looking at eleven, on a real house, at no charge.

The number sits beside the proof. She does the division herself, while the evidence is still on screen.

Why it works: an anchor only anchors against something visible. Eleven renders on the left and “three for $329” on the right is one glance instead of two scrolls.
