What Usually Happens Instead

Eliminating Ten Colors Is Not How This Normally Goes.

Before that, they were exactly where most people are.

Two people who agree on everything else, standing in a driveway holding paint chips. It starts as the fun part of the project. Then the chips come home and they multiply. One of you likes the warmer one. The other is certain it will look pink. Four of them get taped up beside the front door, and for weeks you walk past them, and every time the light shifts your mind shifts with it. Eventually nobody raises it, because raising it restarts the argument. The house stays the color it already is.

And you have already tried everything you are supposed to try
  • Weeks of research. It narrowed the field to a shortlist. It told you nothing about how any of them behaves at the size of a house.
  • Sample patches on three different walls. A patch that size cannot tell you how the whole house will behave. The most common regret afterwards is not having painted them bigger.
  • Asking at the paint store. Staff can advise on color. They have never stood in front of your house, in your light, on your street.
  • Polling friends and neighbours. What you get back is agreement about a card, not about a house.
  • Comparing the finalists side by side. Each one eliminated for an undertone that only showed itself in your own lighting.

None of that is carelessness. It is the industry’s own best advice, followed properly, and it still ends with a color nobody is certain about. A two-foot patch and three thousand square feet of the same paint do not behave the same in your light — the only thing that predicts your whole house is your whole house.

What It Buys You

The One Thing Your Neighbours See Every Single Day

None of this is visible on the day the crew leaves. All of it is visible in year six.

  • Your stucco holds the paint instead of shedding it. Sprayed paint lands on the peaks of the texture and never reaches the valleys. Back-rolling works the wet coat down into the surface, so the film bonds to the wall rather than sitting on the high points and peeling off a facade you just paid five figures for.
  • The color you approved is the color in year six. Paint thinned to stretch a bucket lets UV through to the substrate, and chalking and fade follow. Two coats at full wet-film thickness reach the dry film the manufacturer designed the product around.
  • Repairs disappear instead of becoming scars. A patch that is not floated back to match the texture reads as a smooth streak across a textured wall in raking afternoon light — visible from the street, permanently.
  • Your next repaint is a repaint, not a rebuild. Paint does not fix rot, it conceals it until it is worse and costs more. Wood is repaired before anything is coated over it.
  • Two separate companies stand behind the same wall. Sherwin-Williams Emerald and Duration carry the manufacturer’s own limited lifetime warranty on the coating. VIP’s 2-year written warranty covers labor and materials on top of it.
  • The flaws get found by us, in daylight. Low-angle light is what exposes lap marks, thin spots and misses. The work is inspected under it at completion, which is why you are not the one who notices a week later.
  • You keep living in your house while it happens. Cars, windows, roof tiles, plants and your neighbour’s driveway come out the way they went in, and the site is clean every evening. Your weekend is not a job site.
  • Someone specific answers for the result. The founder walks the finished project and signs off on it, so there is a name attached to the work and a person to talk to about it.
