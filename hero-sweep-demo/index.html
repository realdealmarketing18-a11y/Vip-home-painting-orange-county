Answering the real question: how do we tell the deliberation story without a film?

The commercial existed to prove one thing — that they did not guess. They saw the house in every colour, and only then chose. That story does not need motion picture. It needs evidence, and the evidence is already sitting in viz-photos.

Why the window plays a reel now, not a before/after

A two-state before/after proves the outcome. It says nothing about the decision — and the decision is what a regret-averse buyer is actually frightened of. So the window now dissolves through four candidate palettes, naming each one, and the fifth arrives by wipe. Deliberation, then decision, in nine seconds.

Then the handle appears and the visitor can drag between the last two candidates themselves — which is precisely the moment the real client was in.

On your headline idea

Right instinct, wrong slot. “Discover how they visualized many color schemes prior to painting” makes the process the hero, and a process does not sell to someone afraid of a mistake — the removed risk does. It also spends the h1 on a claim the window proves by itself four seconds later.

Your own OFFER.md settles it: “Claiming beats nothing. Demonstrating beats claiming.” So the h1 keeps the outcome, and the deliberation moves into the line directly above the window, where it works as a caption for what you are about to watch.
