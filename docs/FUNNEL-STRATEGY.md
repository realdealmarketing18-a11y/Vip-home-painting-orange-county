# VIP HOME PAINTING — SALES PAGE & FUNNEL STRATEGY

**A standalone brief.** Written to be handed to another agent or writer with no access to this
repo. Everything needed to execute is in here. Where a number is unverified it says so —
**do not quietly promote an unverified number into copy.**

Business: VIP Home Painting · Anaheim-based · exterior and interior repainting of luxury
residential homes across Orange County · phone **(909) 312-5400** · viphomepainting.com

---

## 1. THE ONE INSIGHT EVERYTHING FOLLOWS FROM

**The buyer is regret-averse, not price-sensitive.**

They can afford the repaint. What they cannot afford is spending five figures on a colour they
end up hating, on a house the neighbours look at every day, in a community where standards are
visible from the sidewalk.

We do not sell paint. We do not even sell craftsmanship. **We sell the removal of a decision
risk.**

The verbatim customer language that proves it, from a homeowner forum thread (real quotes, not
invented):

> *"Frankly I've been feeling sick about it, because it is so permanent and I tried so hard to
> get this right."*
>
> *"Do I just sneak out when nobody's looking and repaint the entire thing Alabaster one panel
> at a time? Will I just get used to it?"*
>
> *"I really determined not to make a rookie mistake on this."*

The feeling underneath is **public, permanent, self-inflicted failure** — not "I dislike the
colour" but "I did this to myself, in front of everyone, and I cannot undo it."

**The aspiration is competence, not beauty.** She is not dreaming about the house. She is
dreaming about not having failed at the house. Every competitor aims at beauty. Aim at
competence.

→ **Never lead with price or discounts. Lead with certainty.**

## 2. THE OFFER

> **A complimentary 30-minute consultation in which our design team renders your actual home in
> every candidate colour scheme — plus a fully itemised estimate — so you approve a colour you
> have already seen on your own house.**

Included: 30-minute in-home consultation · your actual elevation rendered in every candidate
scheme · palette guidance matched to your community's architecture and light · itemised
estimate (prep, primer, coating, trim, accents, line by line) · HOA / design-review submission
package prepared as part of the project · 2-Year Warranty on labour and materials.

### Why it works

A wrong-colour repaint costs **the entire project again**. And almost nobody who regrets their
colour ever looked at it on their own facade first — they chose from a two-inch swatch under
showroom light. The offer eliminates the single most expensive mistake in the category before a
gallon is tinted. That is not a discount or a bonus. **It is risk removal, and risk is what
this buyer is actually paying to avoid.**

### Why it is defensible

Across ~50 painters surveyed in Irvine and Anaheim, **zero** carry a single review mention of
visualization, colour consultation, or seeing the result beforehand. Every competitor tag is
price, speed, tidiness, professionalism — table stakes.

One national competitor *claims* visualization on their Irvine page. **There is no tool on the
page.**

> **Claiming beats nothing. Demonstrating beats claiming.**

This is the only genuinely uncontested position we hold. Protect it: every page leads with it,
the interactive visualizer appears on every page, and no headline buries it below a feature.

## 3. THE TWO BUYERS

### B2C — the homeowner

Owners of $1.5M–$9M+ homes in design-review, often guard-gated communities. Dual-income
professionals, established families, empty-nesters upgrading in place.

**What they fear**, in order:
1. Choosing a colour that looks wrong at full scale — the single biggest fear
2. Being judged — by neighbours, by the HOA, by themselves every time they pull into the drive
3. Crews who treat a $4M estate like a tract repaint — overspray, mess, strangers, noise
4. A vague lump-sum quote that grows mid-project

**What convinces them:** seeing their *own* home in the colour before committing · itemisation
(it reads as competence, not cheapness) · evidence we understand their specific community's
architecture and light · someone else handling the design-review paperwork.

**Where they look:** Google organic — **not** the map pack. They research; they do not search
"near me". Also Nextdoor and neighbourhood groups, referrals, Houzz/Yelp for verification.

### B2B — the HOA board or property manager

Reframe the identical offer:

> *"Your board can show residents the finished clubhouse before the vote."*

The emotional register is **political cover**. A board spends other people's money under
scrutiny and gets voted out over bad calls. Showing the render first removes the risk from the
vote.

**This is a stronger pitch to a board than to a homeowner — and no competitor makes it.**

CTA differs: homeowners **book** ("Claim Complimentary Color Consultation"); boards **procure**
("Request a Bid").

### 4.2 The page spine — section order, and why

Order is not cosmetic. It mirrors how a regret-averse buyer de-risks a decision.

1. **The Reveal (hero).** The visualizer, immediately. A real house transforming. Not a stock
   photo, not a value proposition paragraph. The demonstration *is* the hook.
2. **Name the fear.** State the risk plainly before offering the fix. Never pretend the
   decision is easy.
3. **The method, as steps.** The four-step visualizer, named and numbered (§4.3).
4. **Itemised pricing.** Published, line by line. Competitors publish nothing.
5. **Proof stack**, in this order — visualization, itemisation, materials and method,
   factory-finish cabinets, white-glove site protocol, warranty and licensing.
6. **Community specificity.** The architecture and light of *that* community.
7. **CTA + phone**, repeated.

### 4.4 SEO posture — three findings that should change what you build

- **Do not buy links.** Domain authority barely matters in this market. A competitor ranks #3
  in Anaheim at DR 0.2; VIP at DR 0 is level with page one, not behind it. **The gap is page
  count, not authority.**
- **AI engines cite blog posts, not sales pages.** One competitor has 313 citations; a single
  article earns 55 while hundreds of landing pages earn none. The engines that matter are
  **Copilot (~50%) and Google AI Mode (~36%)** — not ChatGPT. Copilot runs on Bing, so **Bing
  Places and Bing Webmaster Tools beat any ChatGPT tactic.** Build the article page type.
- **Reviews are written by the ~3% who already bought.** They are useful for objections,
  useless for fears. **Copy targets the ~97% who are stalling.** Their language comes from
  homeowner-forum research, not from your own reviews.

Two more: **no competitor has community-level pages** (that is the wedge), and **directories
own about half of page one** — so rank the directory listings as well as the pages.

## 5. THE THREE-STEP CONVERSION FUNNEL

Split by **audience temperature**, not by topic. Each stage is a short vertical cut that also
runs hook → demonstration → CTA *inside itself*.

### Stage 1 · COLOUR — cold

- **Audience:** cold. Orange County homeowners, $2M+ home value, interests in architecture and
  interior design.
- **Objective:** video views / ThruPlay. **No offer.**
- **Content:** the fear named, then all 11 schemes sweeping across one real house.
- **Headline beats:** *"We start with your house."* → *"Eleven colors. One house."*
- **CTA:** soft, or none. A cold audience is not buying a consultation — they are recognising
  themselves.
- **Why no offer:** asking a stranger to book is what makes a painting ad feel like a painting
  ad. Recognition first.

### Stage 2 · THE UPGRADES — warm

- **Audience:** watched 50%+ of Stage 1, or used the visualizer on site.
- **Objective:** landing page views. Also raises order value.
- **Content:** lighting → texture and material → garage doors, each shown on the chosen scheme.
- **Headline beats:** *"The colour is only the start."* → *"The right lighting."* → *"Texture
  and material."* → *"The finishing touches."*
- **Why it exists:** colour is the first decision, not the only one. This is where average
  project value grows, and no competitor advertises it at all.

### Stage 3 · THE BUILD — hot

- **Audience:** site visitors, 30 days. Engaged with Stage 1 or 2.
- **Objective:** leads.
- **Content:** the chosen scheme → **real masking footage** → **real painting footage** → CTA.
- **Headline beats:** *"You choose what you've already seen."* → *"Then we mask it."* → *"Then
  we paint it."*
- **CTA:** "Complimentary 30-minute consultation" + phone.
- **Why it closes:** it completes the loop from render to real house. The masking shot is the
  proof of care; preparation *is* the finish.

### 5.1 Budget and sequencing

| | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objective | ThruPlay | Landing page views | Leads |
| Budget split | 50% | 30% | 20% |
| Destination | — | the page | the page, **at the pricing band** |
| Primary metric | 50% view rate | CTR + time on page | cost per consultation booked |

**Run Stage 1 alone for the first week.** Stages 2 and 3 have nobody to talk to until the
retargeting pool exists.

### 5.2 The rule that makes or breaks it — message match

Stage 1 ends on a line that is **also the lede of section two on the landing page.** Keep it
that way. If the ad and the page open on the same sentence, the click feels like *continuing*.
If they don't, it feels like being sold to twice.

Point Stage 3 at the page **with the pricing band in view**, not the top, so the promise in the
ad is the first thing on screen.

### 5.3 Formats

9:16 (Reels, Stories, TikTok) · 1:1 (feed) · 16:9 (YouTube, site loop). Build all three. The
1:1 is usually the best-composed; the 9:16 must keep anything readable out of the bottom
~300px where platform UI sits.

## 6. HARD RULES — non-negotiable, these have already caused live errors

**Banned words in all customer-facing copy:**

- **Never "free"** → always **"complimentary"**. But reword idioms rather than swapping words:
  "Stress-Free" became "Stress-Complimentary" once, which is not English. It is **"Concierge
  Experience."**
- **Never "AI"**, "automated", "algorithm", "machine learning" → **"our design team"** /
  **"Custom Visualization Service"**.
- Never "cheap", "affordable", "discount", "budget", "save money".
- Never "stunning" or "jaw-dropping" — these read downmarket to this buyer.

**Facts that must never be wrong:**

- **Phone is (909) 312-5400** and nothing else.
- **Tagline is "Visualize It. Love It. Paint It."** — nothing else. Two retired variants are
  still in circulation on old assets: *"See It. Love It. Paint It."* and *"Visualize it. See
  it. Paint it."* Both are wrong. The line reads naturally in every version, which is exactly
  why the wrong one slips through.
- **Warranty is 2 years.** A major competitor also advertises 2, so it is parity, not an edge
  — **never write "longer than the competition."** Carry it in the proof stack, never in a
  headline.
- **Pricing is framed as "VIP starts at $4.75 per square foot of paintable surface."** Never as
  an average, never as "Irvine averages $4.75."
- **Base is Anaheim.** Never publish an address in a city VIP does not operate from.

**Absolutely no review or rating claims.** VIP has **9 reviews** and the rating is unconfirmed;
competitors have 55+. No `aggregateRating` schema, no star counts, no "5-Star Rated", no review
totals. A false rating has already reached a live page twice.

**Never invent** street names, HOA rules, client stories, or reviews.

## 7. WHAT IS OFF-LIMITS AND WHY

- **The $12,000–$15,000 repaint figure.** It is marked `[VERIFY]` — worked illustratively from
  the published rate, not from job data. Replace it with a real closed job before any page or
  ad uses it. The safe version is **"costs the whole job twice"**, which needs no citation and
  holds at any price point.
- **Quoting the homeowner-forum posters.** Their words are the *source of the angles*, not
  testimony VIP owns. Quoting them would present a stranger as a VIP client.
- **Naming any client as a VIP client** until their status is confirmed in writing and a signed
  likeness/testimonial release is on file. A sales page naming a family is one exposure; a paid
  ad doing it is a different one. Default to describing the work, not the person: *"A Newport
  Beach estate · Custom Visualization Service session."*
- **Years in business and crew size.** Unverified.

## 9. THE TEST TO APPLY TO EVERY PIECE

> **Could a competitor put their logo on this unchanged?**

If yes, it is generic and it is wasted. Rewrite until the answer is no.

The second test, for the ads specifically: **does it demonstrate, or does it claim?** Everyone
in this category claims. The entire strategic advantage is that VIP can show eleven renders of
one real house in ten seconds. Lead with the demonstration every time.
